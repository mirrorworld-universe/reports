Performance Framework
Context

What does “valuable work” mean today?

A lot of things we used to spend time on, writing reports, executing tasks, syncing information, are already being replaced by AI, and this trend will only accelerate.

The question is no longer how well we execute, but what kind of value we can still uniquely provide.

Judgment
Being able to decide what is worth doing and what is not.
Creativity
Being able to propose new directions instead of just following instructions.
  • If someone only focuses on execution without thinking, deciding, or creating, it becomes harder to stay competitive.
Purpose

The purpose of this framework

  • We are not trying to “evaluate people” in a traditional sense.
  • It is not a scoring tool designed to categorize or judge people mechanically.
  • We are defining what “excellent” looks like in this team, in this era.
01
A behavioral guideline
It tells us what kind of behavior we value as a team.
02
A feedback mechanism
It helps each of us understand where we are and how we can improve.

Instead of focusing on scores, what matters is whether daily work reflects the kind of people we want to become.

Alignment

Why one unified framework?

  • We are not creating different evaluation systems for engineering, design, GTM, or any other function.
  • AI is breaking down role boundaries very quickly.
  • If we still define ourselves by “this is not my responsibility,” we limit our own potential.
Signal we want to send

By using one unified framework, we expect everyone to go beyond their role, not be defined by it.

Framework

Three core evaluation dimensions

Dimension 01
Ownership
Take responsibility, execute decisively.
Core question: Do you truly own outcomes, not just finish assigned tasks?
Dimension 02
Learning & Sharing
Keep growing, share openly.
Core question: Are you truly growing, and does that growth create value for the team?
Dimension 03
Creative
Innovate boldly, serve the goals.
Core question: Do you have the two most critical capabilities in the AI era, creativity and judgment?
Dimension 01

Ownership

Measures whether you truly own outcomes, not just finish assigned tasks.

Execution’s meaning has changed. It is no longer “I can get this done,” but “I can judge what is worth doing, and drive it to completion.”

Expected behaviors
Proactively identify problems
If you spot a problem, create an issue in Linear with context and expected outcomes.
Follow through to completion
Creators and assignees are both responsible for completion rate.
Quantify goals and own results
Connect delivery to core metrics such as new users or conversion steps.
Bring a proposal
Think through possible solutions before discussing, do not hand the problem away.
Dimension 02

Learning & Sharing

Measures whether you are truly growing, and whether that growth creates value for the team.

In the AI era, your ceiling depends on how quickly you learn new things and put them into practice.

Did you learn?
Did you apply it?
Did you form a best practice?
Did you share it?
Expected behaviors
  • Use AI or a new method to solve a problem you previously could not solve, and turn it into a reusable best practice.
  • Proactively host or join a learning session to share valuable best practices with the team.
  • Share useful tools, methods, or insights in the Sharing channel in real time.
  • Bridge information gaps across teams so useful information does not stop with you.
Dimension 03

Creative

Measures whether you have the two most critical capabilities in the AI era, creativity and judgment.

Creative is the key dimension that differentiates excellent from average.

An average person can complete tasks, but may not know what to do, or how to do it better.

Expected behaviors
  • Make judgments on what is worth doing and why.
  • Explore new paths instead of relying only on old processes.
  • Articulate contribution to company goals such as growth, conversion, efficiency, or product experience.
  • Drive implementation through experiments, MVPs, or A/B tests.
  • Even unsuccessful attempts have value when there is clear retrospective and learning.

Operating Cadence

Monthly execution cadence

First work week
Monthly goal setting
Monday to Wednesday

Create a monthly goal issue in Linear, including 1 to 3 core tasks for the month, quantified as much as possible. It becomes effective after asynchronous confirmation with the leader.

Last work week
Monthly review
Wednesday to Friday
  • How much of your core goal did you complete this month? What did not go well, and why? (Ownership)
  • What did you learn this month, how did you apply it, and did you share it with the team? (Learning & Sharing)
  • What did you do this month that demonstrated creativity or judgment, and which company goal did it serve? (Creative)
Quarterly Outcome

Quarterly evaluation and bonus

Quarterly score calculation

Final score = (M1 score + M2 score + M3 score) ÷ 3

Employees receive only the final score, and will not see dimension weights or detailed calculation.

Calibration meeting
  • Time: first week after the quarter ends
  • Participants: all leaders
  • Duration: 60 to 90 minutes
  • Topics: align standards, large disagreements, high and low performers, cross-team contributions
Bonus payout

Baseline is 0.25 months of salary, adjusted by final quarterly score.
